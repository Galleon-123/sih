the word “wallet” for a moment. Think of welfare as a small support fund for the workers, managed by the cooperative.

Who gives the money?

Customer → pays for service

Suppose the service costs ₹500.

Your cooperative can decide that a small fixed amount, say ₹20, from each completed service goes toward the Worker Welfare Fund.

So:

Customer pays ₹500
→ Worker earns ₹450
→ ₹20 → Worker Welfare Fund
→ ₹30 → other cooperative/platform charges

The exact amounts are just an example—you would decide the actual revenue-sharing rule with the cooperative.

Where does that ₹20 go?

It goes into a fund managed by the cooperative/federation.

For example:

Worker Welfare Fund = ₹50,000

This money is not individually given to the worker after every job.

Instead, it can be used according to cooperative rules for things such as:

emergency financial assistance
worker insurance support
training/upskilling
safety equipment
other worker welfare benefits
Then where does the worker see it?

In the worker app, have a separate:

Welfare

For example:

My Welfare Contributions
₹20 — today's job
₹180 — this month
₹2,400 — total contributed

And perhaps:

Benefits

Insurance: Active ✅
Training support: Eligible ✅

So the worker can see their contribution and eligible benefits, but they don't simply withdraw the welfare amount like normal earnings.

Very important distinction

Worker Wallet = YOUR MONEY

Money you earned from jobs.

Welfare Fund = WORKER SUPPORT MONEY

Money pooled by the cooperative to provide welfare/benefits to workers.

So the flow is:

Customer → Service payment → Cooperative allocates a predefined amount → Welfare Fund → Worker benefits

That is the cleanest way to explain it to an SIH judge. Your current PPT mentions worker welfare/insurance in the problem context, but it does not define this exact fund mechanism, so present it as your proposed feature, not as an existing requirement.


Below is the full explanation of every important **customer/user app feature**—what it does, why it matters, and how it handles real situations.

## 1. Language, login, and accessibility

### Language selection

The user chooses English, Hindi, or a regional language before using the app. Every screen, service category, booking step, and notification appears in that language.

Why it matters: many household-service customers, especially elderly users, may not be comfortable with English-only apps.

### OTP login

The customer logs in with their phone number and an OTP. This creates a verified booking identity without requiring a complicated password.

Fallbacks:

- Resend OTP if it expires.
- Voice-call OTP if SMS is delayed.
- Allow browsing as guest, but require login before payment or booking.

### Voice-guided and accessibility mode

The app can read important steps aloud, offer large text, high-contrast colours, and big buttons.

Why it matters: it makes the app usable by elderly users, users with low literacy, and people with visual difficulty.

---

## 2. Home screen and service discovery

### Location selection

The home screen shows the current area/address. The user can allow GPS access or enter an address manually.

Why it matters: worker matching depends on location.

Fallback: if GPS is unavailable, the user enters city, locality, landmark, and house number manually.

### Service-category grid

The app shows simple service cards:

- Plumber
- Electrician
- Cleaner
- Carpenter
- Painter
- Caregiver
- Gardener
- Driver
- Appliance technician

The user should not need to search through long menus.

### Search bar

Customers can search for a service directly, such as “fan repair,” “bathroom cleaning,” or “elder care.”

### Smart Service Assistant

Instead of requiring the user to know the correct category, they can type or speak their issue.

Examples:

- “My tap is leaking” → Plumber
- “The socket is sparking” → Electrical Emergency
- “Need house cleaning tomorrow” → Cleaner
- “My father needs assistance” → Caregiver

For the hackathon, this can work using simple keyword matching. In the future, AI can classify the issue more accurately.

|

## 3. Service details and price transparency

### Service details screen

Before booking, the customer sees:

- Service description
- What is included
- Estimated duration
- Starting price
- Typical service steps
- Ratings and reviews
- Safety note, if required

Example: an electrical service page may say, “Do not touch exposed wires. Switch off the main supply if safe to do so.”

### Estimated price

The app shows an estimated price range before the customer confirms.

Example:

> Inspection ₹99 + repair charges based on work required.

Why it matters: price transparency reduces disputes and builds trust.

### Cooperative trust information

The page explains that workers are verified through Labour Cooperative Societies, not random unverified freelancers.

This is one of your key differences from normal gig platforms.

---

## 4. Booking feature

### Book now

The customer requests the nearest available worker immediately.

Best for normal or urgent requirements.

### Schedule for later

The customer chooses a future date and time.

Example: “Book a cleaner this Sunday at 10 AM.”

This helps the platform balance worker availability and reduces failed bookings.

### Emergency booking

A separate emergency flow is used for urgent cases:

- Water leakage
- Electrical fault
- Appliance breakdown
- Elder-care need
- Lock/carpentry issue

The app prioritises urgent matching and informs the cooperative organiser if no worker is quickly available.

### Address and landmark

The customer provides house number, street, locality, landmark, and map pin.

Why it matters: Indian addresses can be difficult to locate precisely, so landmarks are useful.

### Issue description and photo upload

The customer can describe the problem and upload a photo.

Example: uploading a photo of a broken tap helps the plumber bring the right tools.

### Multiple-worker request acceptance

The customer can request more than one worker.

Example: an apartment association may request five cleaners for a community-cleaning drive.

### Service bundle

The user can create one booking for multiple related tasks.

Example: “Plumbing + electrical inspection” for a house before moving in.

---

## 5. Worker matching

### Nearby verified-worker matching

After booking, the system finds an appropriate worker based on:

- Location
- Skill
- Availability
- Service category
- Emergency level
- Rating
- Fair-work allocation

### Worker Trust Card

Before confirming, the customer sees:

- Worker name and photo
- Service skill
- Experience
- Rating
- Completed jobs
- Distance and estimated arrival time
- Cooperative name
- KYC verification
- Skill certificate
- Welfare/insurance coverage

This reassures the customer that the worker is reliable and officially connected to a cooperative.

### Why this worker was matched

The app should explain the match in simple language:

> “Matched because the worker is nearby, verified for plumbing, currently available, and has a strong service record.”

This makes the matching system transparent.

### Fair-work allocation

The app should not always assign every job to the highest-rated worker. The system balances jobs across qualified cooperative workers.

Why it matters: it protects worker income and prevents less digitally visible workers from being excluded.

The customer only sees a simple message; the fairness logic happens in the backend.

---

## 6. No-worker available feature

This is very important. The app should never simply say “Service unavailable” and end the journey.

### Expand search radius

If no worker is found within 3 km, the app searches farther away.

### Partner-cooperative search

If the local cooperative has no available worker, the platform searches workers from another cooperative in the federation network.

This is a major advantage of a cooperative federation model.

### Earliest available appointment

If no immediate worker exists, the user sees the next available slot.

Example:

> “All nearby electricians are currently busy. Earliest verified worker: today at 5:30 PM.”

### Priority waitlist

The user can join a priority queue and receive a notification if a worker becomes available.

### Organiser intervention

For emergencies, the app sends the request to the cooperative organiser dashboard. The organiser can manually assign a worker or contact another cooperative.

### Replacement worker

If an accepted worker cancels, is heavily delayed, or does not arrive, the system begins reassignment automatically.

This turns a failed match into a managed recovery process.

---

## 7. Live tracking and job management

### Booking-status timeline

The customer sees the complete status:

1. Booking confirmed  
2. Worker assigned  
3. Worker is on the way  
4. Worker arrived  
5. Work started  
6. Work completed  

This removes uncertainty and reduces unnecessary customer calls.

### Live location and ETA

During travel, the user can see the worker’s estimated arrival time and map location.

For the hackathon, a mock moving map or status update is enough.

### Masked contact

The customer can call or chat with the worker without exposing personal phone numbers permanently.

### Start-work OTP

When the worker arrives, the customer gives a one-time OTP to begin the work.

Why it matters: it confirms the right worker has reached the correct customer.

### Extra-work approval

Sometimes a small repair becomes a larger job. The worker submits a revised cost with explanation and photo evidence.

The customer must approve it before the additional work begins.

This prevents surprise charges.

### Completion OTP

After the work is done, the customer confirms completion using another OTP.

This protects both sides: the worker has proof of completed work, and the customer cannot be charged before service completion.

---

## 8. Emergency and safety features

### Emergency Help button

This should be highly visible on the home screen.

It is for urgent but non-police/non-medical situations such as a major water leak, electrical fault, appliance failure, or urgent elder-care support.

### Safety guidance

Before a worker arrives, the app shows simple safety instructions.

Example:

> “For an electrical fault, do not touch exposed wires. Switch off the main power only if it is safe.”



---

## 9. Payment and fair-wage transparency

### Payment methods

The app supports:

- UPI
- Card
- Wallet
- Cash after service

For the hackathon prototype, UPI can be simulated as a successful payment screen.

### Fair Payment Breakdown

After payment, the customer sees exactly where the money goes.

Example for ₹500:

- ₹400 — worker wage
- ₹50 — worker welfare and insurance fund
- ₹30 — cooperative operations
- ₹20 — platform maintenance

This is a major SIH feature because it proves the platform is cooperative-first, not commission-first.

### Digital invoice

The user receives an invoice containing:

- Booking ID
- Customer name
- Worker name
- Cooperative name
- Service details
- Date and time
- Payment method
- Total amount
- Fair-payment breakup

### Cash confirmation

If the customer pays cash, both customer and worker confirm the amount in the app to avoid future disputes.

### Payment failure handling

If payment fails or remains pending:

- Do not create a duplicate charge.
- Allow retry or another payment method.
- Create a payment-verification ticket if money was deducted but not reflected.

---

## 10. Rating, warranty, and complaints

### Rating and review

After service completion, the user can give:

- 1–5-star rating
- Written review
- Quick feedback tags:
  - On time
  - Skilled
  - Professional
  - Respectful
  - Fair pricing

### Rebook worker

The customer can book the same preferred worker for future jobs if they are available.

### Service warranty

For selected services, the app offers a warranty period.

Example:

> “Plumbing repair covered for 7 days.”

If the same issue returns within the warranty period, the user requests a free revisit or cooperative review.

### Complaint portal

The user can report:

- Worker did not arrive
- Worker was late
- Wrong worker arrived
- Poor service quality
- Overcharging
- Payment issue
- Safety concern

They can upload photo/video evidence and track complaint status.

### Cooperative mediation

Unlike a standard marketplace, the cooperative organiser acts as a mediator. The app shows complaint progress:

1. Complaint received  
2. Under cooperative review  
3. Worker/customer contacted  
4. Resolution offered  
5. Closed  

---

## 11. Notifications and support

### Notification centre

The customer receives notifications for:

- Booking confirmation
- Worker assignment
- Worker arrival
- Delay or cancellation
- OTP reminders
- Payment receipt
- Complaint updates
- Warranty expiry
- Recurring booking reminders

### SMS fallback

If push notifications are disabled, important booking updates are sent by SMS.

### Help centre

The customer can access FAQs, support chat, a callback request, and escalation options.

---

## 13. Offline, privacy, and account settings

### Offline support

The active booking is saved on the phone, even if the network drops.

The app shows:

> “Last synced 4 minutes ago.”

It caches:

- Booking details
- Worker name
- OTP
- Emergency support contact
- Last known status

### Low-data mode

The app can reduce map animation and large images for weak networks.

### Privacy

The customer’s exact address is shown only to the assigned worker. Phone numbers are masked until necessary.

### Settings

The user can manage:

- Language
- Notification preference
- Accessibility mode
- Saved addresses
- Family members
- Payment history
- Logout
- Account deletion request

---

## The one unique feature: Seva Suraksha Guarantee

This is the strongest feature to present to judges.

It is a single “service recovery” system that handles almost every major booking problem.

If something goes wrong, the customer does not get stuck. The app provides the next best action:

| Problem | Seva Suraksha response |
|---|---|
| No worker available | Search partner cooperative / schedule earliest slot / waitlist |
| Worker cancels | Automatic replacement search |
| Worker is late | Revised ETA / replacement request |
| Wrong person arrives | Do not share OTP / report identity mismatch |
| Work cost increases | Customer approval required before extra work |
| Payment fails | Retry / verification / support ticket |
| Work quality is poor | Warranty revisit / complaint |
| Customer feels unsafe | Emergency cooperative support |
| Worker no-show | Priority reassignment / organiser escalation |

Your strongest pitch line is:

> “Uniserve Worker is not only a booking app. It is a cooperative-backed service-assurance platform. Every failed or risky situation has a transparent recovery path for the customer, while keeping workers accountable and protected.”
